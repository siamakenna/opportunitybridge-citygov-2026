# Final proposed submission copy

Prepared for owner approval; unpublished local candidate. Portal fields are not automatically changed.

## Solution description

OpportunityBridge helps students and recent graduates turn classroom work into a credible next step toward a public-service career. Its specific barrier is skills translation. A learner may know HTML or spreadsheets yet struggle to connect that experience to an unfamiliar employer duty, choose an honest work sample, and prepare useful questions for an advisor. NACE’s October 2026 self-rating benchmarks identify career-navigation support needs. Distinct class-year groups, rather than longitudinal tracking, support this rationale; they do not measure OpportunityBridge’s effectiveness.

The intended first setting is a college career-services appointment or workshop. A learner arrives with one skill and leaves with a readable preparation plan. The tool is free to use, requires no account, and keeps employer requirements and unanswered questions visible. Colleges could use it before an appointment so the conversation begins with a specific source, proposed evidence and a question the advisor can address.

Career Bridge demonstrates two different connections. In the MPT route, introductory HTML coursework connects to an educational web-resource duty and a proposed accessible library page. In the Vendor Table Technician route, classroom spreadsheet checking connects to vendor-record maintenance and a proposed fictional data-quality memo. These examples help a learner explain transferable work. Applicant experience, enrollment, authorization, availability and employer acceptance of a sample remain questions for current-source review and human verification.

Each plan contains exactly three ordered actions and can be downloaded as Markdown or JSON. The export retains the official source, review date, employer duty, learner declaration, proposed sample and unresolved conditions. Stale or held sources put a current announcement check before work-sample preparation. Closed opportunities require a current alternative. Optional exercises support practice and can produce a fictional artifact. A learner can export the preparation plan without completing a game or claiming a credential.

The contribution is the inspectable connection across those steps. A learner and advisor can examine how a classroom skill relates to a real duty, what evidence could be prepared, and which question still needs a person. Existing discovery and learning services offer valuable capabilities. Comparative usefulness has yet to be tested. Our demonstration shows this complete preparation workflow in one place with portable outputs and explicit uncertainty.

The local candidate passes software and browser checks, including inspected exports for both routes. These establish implemented behavior. Formal unfamiliar-user benefit, advisor time savings, retention and employment effects remain unmeasured. One informal reviewer has been reported. Actual screen-reader and 200% zoom testing remain outstanding. Source coverage is deliberately bounded, and every expanded mapping requires review.

The proposed first pilot involves five consenting adults, five independently double-reviewed sources and a willing advisor. Named staff would cover source review, facilitation, advice, evaluation, maintenance and accessibility. The model requires 24.25 staff hours and $1,415 economic cost. Paid-labor cash cost is also $1,415. Cash falls to $175 only if every modeled labor hour is donated. No host, staff or funding commitment is claimed. Expansion to 25 learners over 60 days requires the first stage’s safety, actionability, accessibility and workload gates to pass and a separate staffing decision.

The pilot would track source correctness, actionable plans, completion and barriers, advisor review time, and optional 14-day follow-through with consent. Immediate success means a safer, clearer next action that an advisor can inspect. Interviews and placements require later follow-up. The proposed evaluator would retain negative findings, missing responses and source failures, and use them to decide whether continued operation is justified.

## Equity statement

OpportunityBridge makes the steps behind a career-preparation conversation visible to learners who may have limited professional contacts. A free, account-free plan can give a student something specific to bring to an advisor, including an official source, an honest example of their work and a question. Whether that reduces a network-related barrier remains a pilot question.

The public demonstration uses fictional information. Saving is off by default, and a learner chooses whether to download or share a plan. The proposed host would offer equivalent advising, accessible consent, paper materials and appropriate assistance. English-only content, limited source coverage and outstanding assistive-technology testing are current limitations.

The pilot would record where people encounter barriers, require assistance or stop. It would preserve missing responses and withdrawals. Participation would be voluntary and separate from services, grades or hiring. A five-person feasibility stage can reveal concrete problems but cannot establish equitable employment outcomes or subgroup parity.
