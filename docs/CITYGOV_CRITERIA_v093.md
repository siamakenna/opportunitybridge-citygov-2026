# Official judging frame

Source:[CityGov Hackathon 2026 rules,section8](https://www.citygov.com/hackathon/citygov-hackathon-2026),read from the rendered official page October 31,2026. Ten equally listed criteria,each0–10, total 100. The page also has a general0–100 wording; the detailed criterion table specifies0–10. Tie-breakers prioritize career impact/equity,then pilot feasibility. This packet uses the detailed table consistently. No official judging result is claimed.

A Barrier clarity:identify a specific,evidenced education-to-employment barrier.
B Usefulness:practical value and understandable use for learners/job seekers.
C Creativity/originality:novel approach or defensible combination.
D Pilot feasibility:realistic people,resources,timeline,costs and risks.
E Career impact/equity:plausible stronger outcomes and access for people lacking networks.
F Civic relevance:meaningful public-service/public-employer connection.
G Sustainability/scalability:credible maintenance,staffing,funding and expansion.
H Measurement:clear success measures and credible tracking.
I Inclusive accessibility:abilities,devices,backgrounds,privacy and bias.
J Presentation:clear,organized,persuasive communication and demonstration.

Assess the demonstrated prototype against these criteria. Defined/potential outcomes are distinct from observed benefits. The official challenge accepts prototypes and service models; a full placement trial is not itself an entry requirement. Missing user evidence and commitments should be weighed where relevant,without turning software tests into career outcomes. Scores are simulated perspectives,not independent humans or official judges.
