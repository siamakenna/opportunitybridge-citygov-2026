# CityGov 2026 submission copy · OpportunityBridge

**Title:** OpportunityBridge — Civic Careers, With Evidence

**Solo team information:** Team Lead name and CityGov profile URL require owner verification. Do not infer registration or identity from Git. See OWNER_ACTIONS.md.

**Problem:** Learners can have useful classroom, research and community experience without knowing how it maps to civic job requirements or what evidence to bring to an advisor. Requirements, skill proof and next steps are fragmented. This is a specific design hypothesis; prevalence and career impact have not been measured.

**Target users:** College students, recent graduates and adult learners; career advisors; municipal workforce and HR staff. The proposed pilot deliberately includes people without established professional networks.

## Solution description

A student may know how to analyze a class dataset, organize a campus event or explain a difficult topic to a peer. The harder step is seeing how that experience relates to a public-service role, which requirements still need checking, and what to bring to a career advisor. OpportunityBridge turns that uncertainty into an evidence worksheet and a practical next action.

The working, browser-only prototype starts with three fictional learner profiles and four clearly fictional civic pathways covering technology, public health, community engagement and sustainability. A visitor chooses a profile, changes declared facts using Yes, No or Not sure, and inspects a pathway. Every criterion retains its exact illustrative source text and distinguishes required conditions from preferred or contextual information. Missing information stays unknown. A potential conflict prompts verification rather than an automated rejection or a promise of eligibility.

The evidence explorer connects five steps: an existing experience, a transferable civic skill, the exact sample requirement, a suggested portfolio artifact, and a question for human verification. For example, a class analysis can become a small anonymized data-quality sample with documented checks and limits. An optional note lets the visitor draft a fictional example. Prompts are visibly suggestions, and notes are labeled unverified. Changing a declared skill updates the translated suggestions, so the tool does not continue presenting a contradicted default as supported experience.

Visitors can download a two-week career plan or a career-advisor handoff packet. The packet includes the selected pathway, current declarations, requirement excerpts, suggested evidence, optional draft notes, open questions and an advisor review checklist. It gives learners a repeatable way to prepare for a conversation even when they have no warm introduction. Sharing remains their decision; the application sends no email and makes no referral claim.

A separate official-resource section connects practice to real searches. Two Maryland government vacancy bulletins were observed on October 26, 2026, with their employer identities, selected requirements, source links and filing deadlines. District of Columbia and Philadelphia links are explicitly search resources, not verified vacancies. Real sources never enter the fictional eligibility engine. Deadline warnings and a source-review policy acknowledge that postings can close or change. The employer's complete current bulletin always controls.

The application needs no account, payment, résumé upload or runtime AI service. Declarations and draft notes stay in the tab and reset on reload. Downloads are created on the device. System fonts avoid remote font requests; reduced-motion support, labeled controls, visible keyboard focus and responsive layouts support access. Ordinary hosting request logs and the separate policies of external links are disclosed. These design choices reduce barriers but are not a claim of certified accessibility or demonstrated equity impact.

A proposed 60-day pilot gives a college career-services office and a municipal workforce partner explicit responsibilities. The target is 25 consenting adults and 25 manually reviewed opportunities. A frozen comparison protocol provides equal starting information for manual and assisted tasks, independent source checks, blinded scoring and honest denominators. The pilot would measure evidence correctness, actionable plans, next-step follow-through, access barriers and staff workload. Its planning budget is $6,240 including illustrative labor, with a $500 direct-cash envelope if staff time is contributed.

This is a new civic workforce implementation, informed by general provenance and reproducibility principles from earlier projects. AI assistance in code, writing, design, research and evaluation is disclosed. Automated regression tests and an internal fixture dry run document software behavior. Independent unfamiliar users remain n=0; no partner, placement, interview gain or performance advantage is claimed. OpportunityBridge's immediate contribution is a transparent bridge between learning, evidence and human advising that can now be tested honestly.

## Equity statement

Informal networks often help people interpret professional language and identify which experiences are worth showing. OpportunityBridge makes that translation visible: a classroom analysis, mentoring activity or community project can be connected to a civic skill, an exact sample requirement, a possible artifact and a verification question. This is an access hypothesis, not evidence that the tool has closed an employment gap.

The public prototype requires no fee, account, uploaded résumé or personal referral. Unknown facts remain unknown rather than silently becoming disqualifications. Related skills are never presented as certification of eligibility. Visitors control optional fictional notes and local downloads; no demographic scoring or contact sharing occurs. Hosting request logs are disclosed.

The proposed college-and-municipal pilot would offer the same opportunities, human advising, participation compensation and accommodation route in both comparison conditions. Printed worksheets, telephone/in-person support and loaned devices would support learners with limited connectivity or different access needs. Consent would be voluntary and separate from access to services. No participant records would appear in the public prototype.

The interface includes labeled controls, keyboard focus, reduced-motion support and responsive layouts. Browser checks cannot establish screen-reader compatibility or universal accessibility; an access specialist and actual users must test those needs before pilot launch. English-only content is a current limitation. Translations require review and funding.

We would report completion, errors, missing data, barriers and workload, including unfavorable results. A proposed cohort of 25 cannot establish subgroup parity or a causal hiring benefit. No independent learner has been evaluated yet, no institution has agreed to participate, and no placement or equity outcome is claimed. The next test is whether learners and advisors find the evidence conversation clearer and more actionable.

## Success metrics (targets, not results)

- ≥90% criterion/state/source correctness; zero false definite eligibility claims.
- ≥80% of task starters produce plans meeting ≥4/5 blinded actionability criteria.
- ≥60% of consenting follow-up learners report their selected next action within 14 days; self-report separated from advisor confirmation.
- Zero unresolved blocking accessibility barriers; median advisor review ≤15 measured minutes.
