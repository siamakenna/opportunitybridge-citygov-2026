PROBLEM & SOLUTION

A student can have useful classroom, research or community experience and still struggle to turn it into a credible public-service application. Job titles vary, qualification language is unfamiliar, and an informal connection often supplies the explanation. OpportunityBridge makes that explanation inspectable: start with an official opportunity, understand the selected requirements, prepare evidence and bring a concrete question to a human advisor.

The working application now puts real opportunities first. Its curated inventory distinguishes two Maryland vacancies, two dated federal research intakes, four ongoing career or search pathways and one expired fellowship intake. These are different kinds of records, not nine open jobs. Every record includes its official source, employer, observed date, application or search destination, selected qualifications, available compensation and location information, materials and limitations. Visitors can filter by sector, location, career stage and deadline, or maintain a shortlist within the current tab.

Source freshness is part of the experience. Passed deadlines move records out of the default view while preserving their provenance. Records checked more than 48 hours ago become previously observed, and positive selected-condition interpretations are suppressed until rechecking. This matters in practice: Coding it Forward’s page still described applications as open, but its stated October 25 deadline had passed. OpportunityBridge follows the deadline rather than reproducing the stale invitation. A static catalog cannot detect every early withdrawal, so the complete current employer source always controls.

Selecting an actual record opens a requirement-to-evidence workspace. Visitors use explicitly fictional learner declarations, inspect short exact source excerpts and contextual summaries, and distinguish required conditions from preferences. Selected, source-grounded rules preserve three states: declared evidence appears consistent, possible conflict, or insufficient information. For example, the Maryland administrative bulletin publishes a college-credit substitution, while six accounting credits in the fiscal bulletin replace only one year of experience. The tool does not silently turn that partial substitution into full qualification. Sensitive conditions, ambiguous equivalencies and unreviewed fields remain questions for the employer.

The next step is practical preparation. Visitors can draft a fictional evidence note, review source-linked application materials, consider a proposed work sample and download a portfolio plan or an application checklist with an advisor handoff. The packet includes the real recruitment identity, current declarations, selected requirement review, unresolved questions and a 14-day action plan that never extends the employer’s actual deadline. Official application destinations remain primary. Optional Google job search, LinkedIn, Handshake and government search links support further discovery without requiring a platform account inside OpportunityBridge or claiming a partnership.

A separate historical gallery makes public-service work tangible. It distinguishes NIH institutional project reports from former Civic Innovation Corps participants’ published accounts. Each record preserves a documented deliverable, limited career information, its source and what remains unknown. Suggested portfolio exercises are labeled OpportunityBridge recommendations. These people are not product users or endorsers, their roles are not the cataloged Maryland vacancies, and their experiences do not establish our placement outcomes.

The original fictional engine remains available in clearly labeled Practice Mode, preserving the tested baseline while keeping actual opportunities central. The app is free, static and account-free, with no résumé upload, runtime model or automatic application. Drafts and shortlists stay in memory and clear on reload; hosting logs and external-site policies are disclosed.

A proposed 60-day campus-and-municipal pilot would test 25 consenting adult learners and 25 double-reviewed opportunities. It includes accountable staff roles, consent, paper alternatives, source governance and a $6,240 illustrative economic budget. Internal software tests and source-backed fixtures document behavior, but independent unfamiliar participants remain n=0 and the manual comparison is unmeasured. Significant AI assistance is disclosed. The immediate achievement is a working, transparent preparation workflow; the next evidence must come from people and institutions using it.

EQUITY STATEMENT

OpportunityBridge addresses an information barrier that informal professional networks often help people navigate: interpreting requirements, recognizing relevant experience and deciding what to ask an advisor. It makes those steps visible without charging a learner, requiring an account or asking for an uploaded résumé. This is a design hypothesis, not evidence that an employment gap has been closed.

Unknown facts remain unknown. Related skills do not become certified eligibility, preferred skills do not replace mandatory experience, and sensitive conditions remain employer questions. The app does not rank people by protected characteristics or demographic proxies. Historical fellows are presented as attributed examples from other programs, never as product testimonials or promises of placement.

The proposed pilot would offer equivalent source information, human advising, participation compensation and accommodations in manual and assisted conditions. Printed worksheets, telephone or in-person assistance and loaned devices would provide alternatives. Consent would be voluntary and separate from access to services; no private participant records would enter the public app.

The interface includes labeled controls, visible focus, responsive layouts, reduced-motion support and printable checklists. English-only content and the absence of an independent screen-reader or accessibility audit are current limits. No independent unfamiliar learners have been tested. We would report errors, missing data, access barriers, unfavorable findings and staff workload alongside completion. A small pilot could test practical usefulness; it could not establish subgroup parity or a causal hiring benefit.

PILOT AND COST: Proposed 60 days, 25 consenting adults and 25 independently reviewed sources. A campus advisor, municipal HR reviewer, content steward, maintainer, evaluator and accessibility reviewer would own intake, source checks, accommodations and evaluation. Illustrative labor: 112 hours / $5,740; optional compensation $375; materials/access reserve $125; total economic cost $6,240. Cash envelope $500 only if labor is contributed. No partner or funding commitment. Double-review all pilot mappings; minimize data and delete linkage/raw feedback within 90 days unless approved policy requires otherwise. Full weekly milestones, consent template, scoring rubric, risks and sustainability are linked in PILOT_OPERATIONS.md and PILOT_PLAN.md.

PROPOSED SUCCESS METRICS: At least 90% criterion/source correctness and zero false definite eligibility statements; at least 80% actionable plans on a five-item blinded rubric; at least 60% reported 14-day next-step follow-through, separating self-report from advisor confirmation; zero unresolved blocking accessibility barriers and median advisor review at most 15 measured minutes. These are targets, not results.
